# Efflux Pumps and Active Extrusion

**Efflux Pumps** are transmembrane transport protein complexes that actively expel antibiotics, toxic metabolites, and biocides from the bacterial cytoplasm and periplasm into the extracellular milieu. By continuously lowering the intracellular drug concentration below inhibitory thresholds, efflux systems prevent the antimicrobial from reaching its target.

## Transporter Superfamilies

Bacterial efflux systems are classified into five major structural superfamilies based on primary sequence homology, energy source, and subunit organization:

1. **Major Facilitator Superfamily (MFS)**:
   - Single-component membrane transporters driven by the proton motive force ($\text{H}^+$ antiport).
   - Exemplified by [[tetA]] (efflux of tetracycline-cation complexes).
2. **Resistance-Nodulation-Division (RND)**:
   - Tripartite protein machines in Gram-negative bacteria spanning inner membrane, periplasm, and outer membrane (e.g., AcrAB-TolC in *E. coli*, MexAB-OprM in *P. aeruginosa*). Confer multidrug resistance.
3. **ATP-Binding Cassette (ABC)**:
   - Primary active transporters energized by ATP hydrolysis.
4. **Small Multidrug Resistance (SMR)**:
   - Small dual-membrane antiporters.
5. **Multidrug and Toxic Compound Extrusion (MATE)**:
   - Energized by $\text{Na}^+$ or $\text{H}^+$ electrochemical gradients.

## Energetics & Substrate Specificity
- **Specific Transporters**: Highly dedicated to a single drug class (e.g., [[tetA]] for tetracyclines).
- **Broad-Spectrum (MDR) Transporters**: Poly-specific cavities capable of capturing diverse amphiphilic or hydrophobic drug molecules.

## Clinical Relevance
- Efflux pumps frequently act in synergy with reduced outer membrane permeability (porin loss).
- Because they can export multiple unrelated drug classes, hyper-expression of efflux systems can generate instantaneous multi-drug resistance phenotypes.
